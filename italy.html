Italy

Itinerary (PDF)

When you love travelling, you're always looking for the next escape. With a crazy amount of vacation days saved and friends who never seem to have any. I decide to take the plunge and solo travel for the first time ever. Here's my 16 days across Rome, Florence, and Venice.

The Orti Farnesiani sul Palatino or Gardens of Farnese upon the Palatine. This hidden gem in the otherwise overcroweded Palatine Hill, had rows of planted Orange trees.

If you know me, you know my obsession for movies. Obviously I had to stop by The Colosseum to live out my Gladiator fantasy. Maximus I was very entertained

There's something peaceful about all the rooftop terraces around Rome. Enjoying the views of the city around, you start to imagine what life used to be like in these streets hundreds of years ago

The Vatican Musuem & Sistine Chapel. Michelangelo's masterpiece The Last Supper painted across the ceiling here. Start to appreciate the art, culture and history even if you're not someone who typically enjoys things like this. Had to sneak a forbidden picture in

Villa d'Este in Tivoli just outside of Rome. The Train to Tivoli is the easy part, getting to the Villa is the hard part. Maps aren't really helpful when all the local signs are in Italian. A highschooler running late to his classes was able to point me in the right direction. Grazie kid!

The Trevi Fountain is such stunning architecture, you can almost overlook the annoying crowd that surround it. Credit to Mr. Giuseppe Pannini in 1762 for the A+ design

Spent the day at Vernazza, one of five classic colorful fishing villages. Built around a natural harbor and a medieval castle tower. Super chill cat and alot of old people. Ate the best seafood linguine in town.

I love Coffee but Italians take it to a whole new level. You walk into these small cafes with office workers standing around the bar, slugging down their espresso shots. Felt like I was insulting the culture everytime I asked for sugar.

The Vatican Musuem has always been on my bucket list. Decided to do a self guided tour to spend as much time as I wanted at the highlights. When you have Rick Steves Audio Europe audio recordings why do you need a tour guide?

My trip landed on Italy's Jubilee 2025. It's a special Holy Year in the Catholic Church that occurs every 25 years. Attracting millions of extra pilgrims and vistors into Rome and the Vatican City. Looking down at St. Peter's Square from the dome of St. Peter's Basilica in Vatican City.

Here's the Aventine Keyhole a secret view of St. Peter's Basilica perfectly framed by the garden hedges. What you don't see is the hundred people waiting in line to take the exact same picture behind me.

I've seen this Sculpture in pictures countless times before, but seeing it in person was a completely different experience. The Rape of Proserpina at Galleria Borghese in Rome, Italy. Carved the hard marble to look like soft skin. You can see Pluto's fingers pressing visibly into Proserpina's thigh.

Honestly, half the effort of the trip was me trying to get decent dating profile photos by myself. Shoutout to my tripod for being the ultimate travel companion and wingman

There's only so much pizza and pasta you can eat before you're craving something else. At this point I wanted anything but Italian food

The Hundred Fountains of Villa d'Este in Tivoli, Italy. This was probably the most beautiful place I've visited in Italy. I had most of the place to myself and was at peace walking around to the sounds of the fountains.

Italians and their love for Vespas. Almost felt like every picturesque scene was made even better having one somewhere in the frame. How else would you know you're in Italy?

You see the Fork and Knife next to the plate? Try not to use that to eat your pizza at a fine dining restaurant. The head chef will lose his mind and yell at you. Pizza was still delicious though, so worth it

Another picture I waited in line over an hour for. Upgrading my selfie game at Chiesa di Sant'Ignazio di Loyola on the other side of the world. Spend some extra time taking that perfect picture for your socials

The YellowSquare Rome hostel was a wild experience. Got to meet alot of new faces and hear things I wish I could forget. Shoutout to Steph and the rest of the roommates here who shared moments, laughs and their stories with me

People usually don't recommend visiting Naples. Graffit filled streets and the strong personality of the locals too much to handle. I loved every gritty thing about the city. Spent countless hours just walking around aimlessly in the back alleyways well past into midnight. Thankfully I didn't get robbed, because the opportunity would definitely be there.

Uffizi Gallery in Florence. The Tribuna of the Uffizi was built as a private Medici "cabinet of wonders" to showcase rare art inside an octagonal room designed around the four elements. The ancient roman loved their naked women and so did Medici and I

It wasn't planned but I somehow ended up spending Valentines Day at Carnevale di Venezia. Can't imagine any place more romantic that you would also want to avoid being a desperate single person. The masks and costumes were super cool

The end of the trip and the best photo I've ever taken in my life. Everything came together perfectly when a Gondala driver rode across by the shaded canal in Venice.
